## Which one applies to you

**The AGPL costs nothing and is almost certainly what you want** if you are:

* an individual, hobbyist, student or researcher;
* an academic or educational project;
* an open-source project that is itself AGPL-compatible;
* a company using nanomem **internally** — running it on your own machines for
  your own purposes, without conveying it to anyone and without offering it to
  users over a network.

**You likely want a commercial licence** if you are:

* offering nanomem, or a service built on it, to users **over a network**. AGPL
  section 13 requires you to offer those users the complete corresponding
  source of your version. If your service is proprietary, that is the clause
  that binds you;
* **distributing** nanomem inside a proprietary product, embedded in a closed
  application, or bundled with software you do not wish to release under the
  AGPL;
* subject to a policy that prohibits AGPL-licensed dependencies.

If you are unsure whether internal use triggers section 13, the practical test
is whether anyone *outside* your organisation interacts with nanomem through a
network. If not, the AGPL is satisfied.

## Patents

The previous licence (`LICENSE.preview-v1.0.md`) reserved all patent rights and
stated that no implied patent licence was granted.

**That reservation does not carry over to the AGPL path, and it cannot.** AGPL-3.0
section 11 grants every recipient a licence under the contributor's essential
patent claims, for the covered work. Distributing nanomem under the AGPL
therefore grants that patent licence to AGPL recipients. This is stated plainly
rather than left ambiguous, because a licence file that grants a patent right in
section 11 while a second paragraph withholds it is unusable by anyone's lawyer.

Patent rights **outside** what AGPL-3.0 section 11 grants are reserved, and a
commercial licence is negotiated separately including any patent terms it needs.
